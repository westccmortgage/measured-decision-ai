Three rules the software cannot break

AI reads. People sign.

Every AI reading wears "Read by AI · not confirmed" until a named person confirms it, line by line. An owner clicking a button never turns a machine's number into a human fact — that is enforced in the database, not in a policy document.

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

If the plans require 14 piles and the record shows 12, that is a conflict only when the capture provably covered the whole area. Otherwise it is "2 not yet evidenced" — a question, not an accusation.

Delivery is not installation.

An invoice proves purchase. A photo proves presence. Neither proves conformity. The record keeps the three apart by construction: "14 required · 14 documented as delivered · 12 visually evidenced as installed."

How a week of oversight works

  1. Upload the plan set.The AI reads printed dimensions, schedules and legends — it never measures a drawing by scale — and produces a takeoff and a component list where every quantity names its source sheet. What the plans don't answer becomes an RFI automatically.
  2. Walk the site with a 360 camera.Room-by-room capture becomes a permanent visual record — viewable in a browser, or walked at full scale in Apple Vision Pro. Originals are preserved untouched.
  3. Drop in the paperwork.Invoices and delivery tickets read themselves into the record as deliveries — never as installation.
  4. Read one page.Required vs delivered vs installed, room coverage, open questions, and a decision log where every entry names the person who decided. Download the workbook; nothing requires you to count, measure, or type a technical value.

What this is not

Not a contractor's estimate: no waste factors, no pricing, no cut optimisation — a verification baseline your estimator can stand on.

No compliance certifications are claimed, and the software never certifies code compliance, structural adequacy, or workmanship. Those judgements belong to licensed professionals; the record gives them evidence.

No savings percentages promised. A pilot measures its own results from the project's record — decisions on record, evidence coverage, questions closed — and those numbers are the case, or there isn't one.
